01 · About

Five years of turning manual, error-prone workflows into governed, automated systems.

I started in mechanical engineering and moved into software because I liked systems I could actually trace end-to-end — a request goes in, a set of rules decides what happens to it, and something observable comes out the other side. That's most of what I've built since: ERP and workflow platforms where the hard part isn't the CRUD, it's the approval logic, the audit trail, and what happens when a downstream service is briefly unavailable.

At SysThink Technologies I helped migrate a legacy manufacturing ERP to microservices — asynchronous messaging over AWS SQS, the Saga pattern for distributed transactions, circuit breakers so one failing service didn't take the rest down with it. At Incedo, I'm now doing the equivalent for wealth management: Java microservices orchestrated with Camunda BPM that move SME account requests through validation, OFAC compliance screening, and approval, for a platform used by advisors at Steward Partners.

I care about the same things whether the domain is a factory floor or a brokerage: correctness under concurrency, clear audit trails, and code a teammate can pick up without a walkthrough.
